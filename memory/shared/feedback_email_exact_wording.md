When the user says what an outgoing email should ask for, the email carries that ask almost word for word, as its own sentence, not paraphrased or folded into a conditional. Before sending a newly worded email, show the user the exact text and get a yes on that text.

**Why:** on 2026-11-09 the user had asked for lawyers to be asked for "an invoice ... 10-30 mins of their time" to review documents. When they then said "send an email to lisa, julie and Jim asking to review the documents and their initial thoughts of winning, and pricing", I wrote and sent "If you bill for the review, please send an invoice", dropping the 10 to 30 minutes. The user had meanwhile written "PLEASE MAKE SURE THE EMAIL reads please send us an invoice of 10-30 mins of your time to review our documents" and "ughhhhh you fucked up". The emails could not be recalled.

**How to apply:**
1. Carry forward every specific term the user set earlier in the same thread of work (amounts, time spans, names) unless they drop it.
2. For a new email text, show the full wording first; send only after the user approves that wording, even if they said "send".
3. Short, plain emails; see [[feedback_lawyer_emails_no_internal_details]].
